### Real climate problem（1421 字符）
```
Track 01 — Climate-smart Tea Farming in Meijiawu.

TeaWindow addresses compound climate risks affecting Longjing 43 tea in Meijiawu (30.21°N, 120.10°E), a core West Lake Longjing production area in Hangzhou, China. Around 70% of local tea gardens grow Longjing 43, an early-budding cultivar with relatively weak drought tolerance.

Early spring growth exposes tender shoots to late frost and sudden cold spells, while summer heat and drought can scorch leaves and affect both current growth and the following spring harvest. Prolonged rainfall and typhoons further complicate harvesting and field management.

The impacts are documented. A 2010 cold event affected about 1.5 million mu (~100,000 ha) of tea gardens across Zhejiang and caused approximately RMB 1.7 billion in losses; in the West Lake area nearly 30% of tea gardens were affected and harvesting was delayed by about ten days. In 2020 and 2022, about 5,000 mu (~330 ha) of tea gardens in the West Lake area experienced heat damage, with some gardens reporting yield reductions of about one third. Hangzhou recorded 60 high-temperature days in 2024, a record.

Users are tea farmers, harvesting teams, cooperatives and tea enterprises, who must decide when to harvest, irrigate, drain fields, provide frost protection or deploy shade. Weather, terrain and vegetation signals are scattered across sources and hard to translate into timely, plot-level actions.
```

### AI's task（1848 字符）
```
TeaWindow turns weather, vegetation and terrain data into daily compound-risk assessments and practical tea-garden actions.

It computes agricultural climate indicators — growing degree days, frost, sudden spring cold, heat stress, consecutive dry days, water deficit and prolonged rainfall. A gradient-boosted decision tree combines temperature, humidity, soil-moisture estimates, precipitation, phenology and temporal features to identify compound risk; ensemble forecasts add uncertainty ranges, and terrain plus NDVI support plot-level interpretation. Transparent rules derive harvest suitability and recommend harvesting or maintenance actions. DeepSeek is optional and only rewrites verified results into accessible Chinese; it is never the source of numbers, and a rule-based fallback always exists.

AI is essential because a single threshold (e.g., minimum temperature alone) cannot capture interactions among frost, heat, drought, rainfall and crop stage. On held-out 2024–2025 data the multivariable GBDT reproduced expert risk labels better than a single-temperature baseline (AUC 0.998 vs 0.78; F1 0.85 vs 0.50), though this is weak-label evaluation, not field accuracy.

Validation: spring frost 2022–2025 (Feb–Apr) vs ERA5 over 357 days — Tmin MAE 1.32°C (95% CI 1.21–1.43) vs 2.08°C persistence; lead-time MAE 0.49/0.67/0.91°C at 1/3/7 days; frost POD 0.709, FAR 0, CSI 0.709 (73 TP, 30 missed). ERA5 is reanalysis, not tea-garden ground truth; next we freeze forecast versions and compare with timestamped plot-level sensors and explicit positive/negative field reports. The same interfaces expose plot-level data (/api/plot, /api/plot/series) and a printable plot health card, so verified numbers reach farmers as an actionable card. Recommendations remain decision support, checked against field conditions and official warnings.
```
